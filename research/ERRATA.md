### SAR science and data facts (Sections 4 and 5, Appendices A and B)

| Brief section | The brief says | What is true |
|---|---|---|
| 5, 'Respect speckle' | 'A single-pixel change of 1 to 2 dB is noise' | Understated by a factor of three or more. At native posting (about 2.5 to 9 looks) the two-date difference of an unchanged pixel has a standard deviation of 2 to 4.3 dB and a 95% bound of about +/-4 to +/-8.5 dB; nearly half the pixels differ by more than 2 dB at 5 looks. 1 to 2 dB only means something per pixel after averaging linear power to roughly 50 to 100 looks (20 m data: 4x4 or 5x5; 10 m data: 8x8 to 10x10). |
| 5, 'There is no universal threshold' | The log-ratio method: 'subtract two dB images after a roughly 50 m mean filter and flag pixels beyond about 1.5 standard deviations of the difference image'; also 'an after/before ratio' | A k-sigma rule flags about 13% of pixels two-sided (6.6% one-sided) on a pair where nothing changed, whatever the filtering, so it ranks pixels rather than detecting change. In the ARSET source it is one-sided, on Sentinel-1 VH, after a focal mean of 50 m radius applied to dB images, and gave 2.35 and 2.95 dB. The ARSET 'ratio' divides two dB images and thresholds at 1.25; it is not a linear power ratio. For NISAR: filter in linear power, threshold one-sided in the expected direction, and publish the share flagged over a stable area. |
| 6.3 and Appendix D sketch D5 (bears on Section 5) | Display COGs are 8-bit dB codes written with overview_resampling='average'; lo and hi are recorded 'so the browser can turn pixel values back into dB' | Overview levels average dB codes: uniform areas decode 0.2 to 0.4 dB low and mixed pixels are off by several dB (10 to 13 dB at edges). The base level is nearest-neighbour reprojected and snaps to a web-mercator zoom (20 m became 25.6 m at 47.9N).
